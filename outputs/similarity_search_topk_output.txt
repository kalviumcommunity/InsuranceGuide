OBSERVATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Increasing k returns more chunks from the same ranked list; it does not
change the order of chunks already returned by a smaller k. Once k
reaches the total number of indexed chunks (3, in this small corpus),
asking for a larger k (k=5 above) stops returning new results and
simply returns everything in the collection - k=3 and k=5 return the
same three chunks in the same order.

Even with placeholder scores, the ranking above is directionally
correct: the query is about fire/storm damage to a house, and the
sample.md chunk (Property Insurance - fire, storms, theft) is the only
one of the three that shares meaningful vocabulary with the query, so
it ranks first. This is why the demo is still useful to inspect even
before the real embedding call is made - but the actual scores that go
into the RAG grounding step must come from real embeddings.
